# R3 evidence register — US (2026-10-24 snapshot)

Figures here are **observations from a stated source and definition**, not a composite narrative. Source IDs are in [source-inventory.csv](source-inventory.csv). Numeric transcription and mechanical validation are in [data/verified-observations.csv](data/verified-observations.csv). Dates below denote **observation periods**; source publication dates are separate.

## E1. Functional labor / nonlabor share
**BLS nonfarm business (BLS-LAB):** BLS reported the labor share of nonfarm-business output was **65.5% in 1947 Q1** and **52.8% in 2026 Q2**, the latter the lowest published observation since 1947. These are percent of sector output, quarterly, not a percentage of total GDP or national income. The series includes compensation and an **imputed labor component of proprietors' income**, with employee wages, benefits, pensions and social insurance costs; it excludes much of general government, private households, farming and nonprofits. [BLS 2026 chart/table](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/labor-share-at-its-lowest-level-52-8-percent-in-second-quarter-2026.htm); [BLS measurement paper](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2017/article/estimating-the-us-labor-share.htm).

**Dangerous apparent corroboration:** FRED [PRS85006173](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PRS85006173) is an **index (2017=100)**, not the percentage labor share. Its 2026 Q2 reading of 93.446 cannot be presented as 93.446% of output. Relative-index changes may be assessed *within the same series*, but the level is not the share.

**Required rival denominator:** BEA [NIPA Table 1.12](https://apps.bea.gov/iTable/?categories=survey&isuri=1&reqid=19&step=2) records compensation of employees, proprietors' income, corporate profits, interest, rental income, taxes and national-income components. A compensation / national income ratio **excludes proprietors' imputed labor** from the numerator, so it cannot replace BLS labor share. BEA gross domestic income uses depreciation (consumption of fixed capital), production taxes and statistical discrepancy; it is also not the same denominator as **net** national income. Separate gross and net versions; no one-for-one 'capital receives the rest' assumption.

**Sensitivity checks remaining:** fixed weights vs changing industry composition; gross vs net value added; corporate vs noncorporate sectors; wage vs employee total compensation vs imputations; post-pandemic volatility; quarterly peaks/troughs vs multi-year averages. The BLS 2017 methodological review explicitly documents the self-employment imputation and variation across sector definitions. No single breakpoint approved.

## E2. Income concentration and tax/transfer definitions
**CBO household series (CBO-2023):** Official September 2026 analysis covers **1979–2023** and ranks households in a consistent CBO historical framework. The top 1%'s share of **income before transfers and taxes** was **9% (1979)** and **16% (2023)**; the share of **income after transfers and taxes** was **7% (1979)** and **13% (2023)**. These are household shares, not the wages of the top 1% of workers and not tax-return AGI. [CBO full report](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62792); [report landing/data](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62761). CBO defines 'before transfers and taxes' as market income plus social insurance benefits; market income includes realized capital gains and business income. 'After' adds means-tested transfers and subtracts federal taxes. CBO’s 'before' therefore is **not** purely labor/capital market income and is **not** an untouched pretax national-income measure.

**Volatility/counterobservation:** the predecessor [CBO 2022 report](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62300) recorded a **top-1% pre-transfer/tax share of 18% in 2022** and **after share 14%**, versus **16% / 13% in 2023**. Changes around 2020–23 particularly reflect realized capital gains, extraordinary fiscal supports and expiry. Avoid asserting a monotonic trend or interpreting a 2022 observation as 2023.

**CBO taxes:** in 2023 the highest income quintile paid **70% of federal taxes** (vs **55% in 1979**) and the top 1% paid **26%** (vs **14%** in 1979) [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62792). These are shares of all **federal** taxes in CBO's allocation, not income tax liabilities and not average effective rates. Include this counterobservation before suggesting income shares imply lower tax contribution.

**Independent BEA lens (BEA-PI):** distributed **personal income** anchored to NIPA Table 2.1 and equivalized by household size, available in published series through 2024. Its 2024 top quintile held **52.8% of personal income**; BEA's **experimental 2025 nowcast** is 52.7%. The latter is not observed administrative microdata. BEA explicitly labels **2024 provisional**, with interval ranges for the top 1% and top 5% because underlying IRS SOI data are delayed. [BEA distribution data / methodology / CSV](https://www.bea.gov/data/special-topics/distribution-of-personal-income). CBO household income before/after federal taxes, BEA personal income and IRS AGI are not interchangeable.

**IRS SOI (IRS-SOI):** ranked **tax returns** and AGI-based percentile shares. A 2010 methodology change expanded the calculation universe (excluded dependent returns and no longer only positive-AGI returns); longer-term tax-policy changes shift business income between tax forms and create breaks. [IRS SOI method explanation](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-individual-income-tax-rates-and-tax-shares). A tax return can be joint; neither 'tax returns' nor 'taxpayers' equals households or adult persons. Observational levels in other studies cannot be appended to CBO without an explicit bridge.

**Census CPS ASEC (CENSUS-2025):** Census reports the 2025 pretax money-income Gini change from 2024 was **not statistically significant**. Its definition excludes noncash benefits and some capital income; Census recalibrated 2024 estimates using updated population controls. [Census 2025](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-289.html). Do not infer statistical significance from two point estimates absent published standard errors.

## E3. Wealth stocks and ownership
**Fed Distributional Financial Accounts (FED-DFA):** combines Financial Accounts aggregates and SCF distributional information, quarterly since **1989 Q3**. At **2026 Q2**, Fed/FRED reports **top 1% held 32.5% of *net worth*** and **bottom 50% held 2.3%** (net-worth percentile groups, percent of aggregate, not seasonally adjusted). The latter series starts at **3.5% in 1989 Q3**. These are **stocks** valued at quarterly market conditions and include liabilities netted from assets. [Federal Reserve DFA](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/dataviz/dfa/); [top 1% WFRBST01134](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WFRBST01134); [bottom half WFRBSB50215](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WFRBSB50215). Earlier June 2026 DFA vintage reported *top 1% 31.6% in Q1 2026* whereas September 2026 revision reports **31.7% in that same quarter**, a concrete warning not to mix vintages. Fed/FRED Q2 2026 data updated **2026-09-18**.

**Denominator guard:** in the Fed wealth dashboard there is also 'share of total **assets**' and 'financial assets' — neither is the share of net worth. A 2026 Q2 top-1% figure of **29.9% of total assets** cannot be used as the 32.5%-of-net-worth observation. A 'top 0.1%' line is a subset of top 1%, not an additional disjoint class.

**2025 SCF (FED-SCF):** 2025 triennial survey and historic tables (1989–2025) released on 2026-10-09 with public data, five imputations and replicate weights; a **sample** measure with standard errors, unlike quarterly modeled DFA allocation. [SCF data and uncertainty guidance](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/scfindex.htm). Recompute group uncertainty with correct implicates/replicates before treating close differences as significant. Survey 'family' differs from the Census household population.

**Ownership is not a measured cash-transfer pathway:** a concentration of equity valuation does not establish how current output divided among wages, depreciation, net profits and payments, or whose realized taxable income rose; equity appreciation may be unrealized. Retirement claims, foreign owners and intermediaries complicate look-through.

## E4. Executive compensation
**SEC original legal and filing basis (SEC-PAY):** Item 402(u) mandates reporting registrant CEO annual total compensation, median **firm employee** pay and ratio for covered issuers, with compliance for fiscal years starting 2017. Registrants can use reasonable sampling and estimation, and may exclude some employees; the SEC cautions about methodological heterogeneity. [SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2015/08/pay-ratio-disclosure); [SEC calculation guidance](https://www.sec.gov/about/division-corporation-finance-guidance-calculation-pay-ratio-disclosure). A documented company-level example reports a **65:1 fiscal 2018** ratio but also states ratios may not be comparable across companies. [Original EDGAR proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937098/000093709819000110/a123118tnetschedule14a.htm).

**Constructed time series (EPI-CEO, secondary, not an SEC table):** the 2026 EPI series for large companies reports **1978 realized/granted 30.7 / 22.8** and **2025 realized/granted 324.7 / 246.9**, expressed as CEO annual compensation to *private-sector production/nonsupervisory worker* compensation, with worker-industry mix controls in some years. It is **not** the same as the SEC firm-median ratio, nor a panel of all US CEOs; earlier years are reconstructed from varying records. [EPI 2025 observations released in 2026](https://www.epi.org/publication/ceo-pay/). The EPI 2025/2026 series revised past values: its earlier 2024 release reported 2024 realized ratio 280.72 while updated 2026 table shows **290.2** for 2024. Preserve release date and methodology; do not silently splice EPI vintages.

**Options:** realized = value of option exercises and vested equity at measurement date; granted = grant-date valuation. Neither necessarily equals cash salary or economic opportunity cost in the same year; stock-market appreciation changes 'realized' compensation.

## E5. Corporate distributions
**S&P DJI disclosed index universe (SP-BB):** Q3 2025 **gross repurchases $249.0 billion**, Q3 2025 **dividends $168.1 billion** and combined **$417.1 billion** (summing rounded figures). Trailing 12 months ended September 2025: repurchases **$1,020.27 billion**, dividends **$664.90 billion** (combined approximately **$1.685 trillion**). Q3 2025 repurchases compared to **$234.6 billion Q2** and **$226.6 billion Q3 2024**. These are S&P 500 **constituents' reported cash expenditures**, not all corporations, and the 12-month number is a rolling **flow**, not wealth or cumulative lifetime return. Data were explicitly labelled *preliminary*. [Originating index-provider press release 2025-12-18](https://press.spglobal.com/2025-12-18-S-P-500-Q3-2025-Buybacks-Post-Modest-6-2-Gain-to-249-0-Billion-After-Declining-20-1-Amidst-Uncertainty-in-Q2-Q4-2025-Expenditures-Expected-to-Post-Similar-Growth%2C-As-2025-Anticipates-a-Record-1-Trillion). Note top 20 issues comprised **49.5% of Q3 2025 repurchases**; sample mix is material.

**Fed Z.1 all corporate (FED-Z1):** [Z.1](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/) distinguishes *transactions* and *positions* and [F.103 nonfinancial corporate business](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/20260319/html/f103.htm) reports **2025 net dividends $1,394.5 billion** (2026-03-19 vintage, billions/year). An earlier 2025 vintage gave materially different historical levels: do not compare unaligned vintage snapshots. Different scope and netting mean F.103 dividends should **not** be summed with S&P 500 dividends or interpreted as the same measure. The wider 'corporate business net dividends' FRED [BOGZ1FA096121073Q](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BOGZ1FA096121073Q) is **$2,304,286 million SAAR in Q2 2026**, which is an annualized rate, not an actual Q2 cash total. [BEA definition of net vs personal dividends](https://bea.gov/help/faq/200).

**Critical rejection:** negative net equity issuance in Z.1 **is not gross buybacks**. It reflects gross issuance, gross redemptions (including acquisitions, retirements and flows across sectors) and classification choices; negative net issuance cannot be equated to cash returned to US households. Buybacks can alter ownership fractions, share counts and EPS; they are purchases of existing claims, not production income in GDP.

## E6. Unions and institutions
**BLS CPS (BLS-UNION):** 1983 comparable base **20.1% of wage-and-salary workers** union members (17.7m), versus **10.0%** (14.7m) in 2025. Sector distinction: 2025 private-sector **5.9%**, public-sector **32.9%** members. Membership is not representation/coverage; 2025 BLS Table 3 reports both. 2025 estimate based on **11 months, excluding October** due to federal shutdown; new **2022 Census industry classification** introduced effective Jan 2025 **without historical revision**: 2025 is not strictly comparable for some industry series. [BLS 2025 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/union2.htm); [sector/industry table and footnotes](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/union2.t03.htm). Earlier than 1983 requires a distinct historical source/population.

**Institutional observations** and dates in [institutions.md](institutions.md) cover NLRA/NLRB, Taft-Hartley, tax-law effective dates, disclosure and the 2023 buyback excise. They do not estimate counterfactual effects of laws on these measured outcomes.

## Interpretive constraints, without a synthesis
There is no observed common 'surplus recipient' in these datasets. National accounts, income distribution, balance sheets and issuers' payouts answer **different** questions. Matching years, populations, income concepts, deflators and aggregate accounting is mandatory before decompositions. Decomposition of average compensation shares does not answer how the median worker fared (R1) or what households could afford (R2). Potential explanatory channels — technology, capital intensity, depreciation, globalization, employment composition, demographics, corporate structure, market power, and bargaining — require separate models and counterfactual tests, not inference from these correlations. See [competing-measures.md](competing-measures.md).
